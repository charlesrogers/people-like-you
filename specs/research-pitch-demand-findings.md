# Research Findings — What Makes a Pitch Card Get Fired

**Commissioned by:** `specs/research-brief-pitch-demand.md` (Charles, 2026-08-23)
**Date:** 2026-08-24
**Method:** Five parallel literature-verification agents plus direct power analysis. Every load-bearing claim carries a link and a study-type label — **FIELD** (real randomized behavior), **LAB** (hypothetical/attitudinal), **META** (meta-analysis), **PLATFORM** (observational platform data, no randomization), **VENDOR** (industry analysis with commercial incentive). Claims that could not be verified against a primary source are flagged, not used. Two agents independently caught *fabricated statistics circulating in this space* (a fake Fader quote, a fake email-spam statistic, a misattributed WTP figure) — everything below survived primary-source checking.

---

## Headline answer

**The variance is in craft, not in targeting. Stop aiming and make every pitch excellent.** The reader × content interaction is probably real but small — the honest literature-wide estimate for PLY's cell (trait inferred from behavioral proxies, matched message compared against a *good generic* card, behavioral outcome) is **r ≈ .14–.17, and d ≈ 0.12 in the only mature field literature (health-message tailoring, N=58,454)**. At 50 readers and ~1,200 fire events, PLY's minimum detectable interaction is **OR ≈ 2.24 (a ~13pp difference-in-differences on a 20% base)** — the literature-calibrated effect (OR ≈ 1.67) sits *below the detection floor*, and Gelman's Type-M result says anything we "find" at that power is expected to be ~3× inflated. Meanwhile craft-side variables have measured field effects at or above the main-effect detection threshold, and — the single most decision-relevant finding of the sweep — **matching amplifies whatever argument quality is already there, in both directions: targeting a weak card is predicted to be *worse* than not targeting it** (Teeny et al. 2021). Craft is not the competing investment; it is the precondition for targeting to have positive expected value at all.

This is not "targeting is fake." It is: at N≈50, targeting is (a) too small to detect, (b) worthless without craft, and (c) already 80% captured by a craft rule the data supports — put universally-valued, concretely-demonstrated qualities in every card, because the best evidence says *anyone's* ideals move readers about as much as *their own* (Sparks et al. 2020, unique-fit test null). Bank the reader data cheaply, keep the style randomization for continuity, and revisit targeting as a Phase-T hypothesis at ~100+ readers, where the within-reader design makes a moderate interaction genuinely detectable.

## F1 · Matz vs Ehrenberg-Bass, resolved

**The Matz result is far weaker than its citation, and its strongest study found nothing on clicks.** Confidence: high.

[Matz, Kosinski, Nave & Stillwell 2017, PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1710966114) (**FIELD**, full text read at [PMC5715760](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5715760/)) — the actual per-study numbers behind "up to 40% more clicks":

| Study | Reach | Click effect | Conversion effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 — extraversion × beauty ads | 3,129,993 | **OR = 1.00 [0.96, 1.04], p = .98 — nothing** | OR = 1.54, carried by **390 purchases** (0.012% base) |
| 2 — openness × crossword app | 84,176 | OR = 1.38 | OR = 1.31, driven **entirely by low-openness readers**; high-openness null (p = .325) |
| 3 — tailored vs standard ad | 534,250 | OR = 1.30 | OR = 1.15 — **not a matching interaction at all**: a better-written ad vs a generic one, i.e. the *craft* arm |

Three published critiques, all verified:
- [Eckles, Gordon & Johnson 2018, PNAS letter](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1805363115) (preprint read in full): users were **not randomly assigned** — Facebook's delivery optimizer chose who saw which ad. Covariate imbalance in Matz's own data at p < 10⁻¹⁸ to p < 10⁻²¹⁷; "biases of 25%, 9%, and 5% are sufficient to account for these results even without differences in effectiveness." An unresolved internal-validity challenge, not a failed replication — no direct replication exists.
- [Sharp, Danenberg & Bellman 2018, PNAS letter](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1805363115): "The two cases where the psychologically targeted ad worked better suggest instead that it was the **creative quality** of these ads that was superior, not their targeting." (A correction later disclosed Ehrenberg-Bass funding from Facebook and competitors — flagged.)
- [Tappin, Wittenberg, Hewitt, Berinsky & Rand 2023, PNAS](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10288628/) (**survey experiment**, N = 5,284, the best-designed head-to-head): microtargeting beat the single best message in one scenario (+2.5pp) and **lost to it in the other** (12.30pp vs 15.42pp, n.s.), with "no evidence that targeting messages by more than one covariate yielded additional persuasive gains."

**The umbrella meta-analysis** — [Joyal-Desmarais et al. 2022, *Psychological Bulletin*](https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000377) (**META**, 702 studies, 5,251 effects, N = 206,482, preregistered; 107-page preprint read in full at [OSF](https://osf.io/8jaqv/download)) — headline r = .20, but every relevant moderator cuts against PLY's cell:

| Moderator | r |
|---|---|
| Trait manipulated by experimenter | .22 |
| Trait directly measured | .17 |
| **Trait inferred from a proxy (= PLY)** | **.14** |
| Matched vs deliberately *mismatched* message | .28–.30 |
| **Matched vs generic message (= PLY's real counterfactual)** | **.17** |
| Self-report behavior main effect | **.08** |

The behavioral *interaction* estimates rest on **5 and 15 studies** (CI for observed behavior [.07, .38]); 64% student samples; effects decay over time; most prediction intervals cross zero.

The Ehrenberg-Bass side is audited in F1b, the creative-share vendor numbers in F1c — with the warning that **both camps' flagship evidence is weaker than its reputation**.

**So for PLY:** the F4 line in the decision memo ("expected effects are big enough to detect at our scale") is **wrong as stated**. Matz's own click-outcome evidence at scale is null; the field-wide honest estimate for our configuration is r ≈ .14–.17; and the best-controlled comparison found the single best message competitive with or better than targeting. F4 should be re-labeled: personality-matched messaging is *a contested, probably-small effect measured against weak baselines* — not the justification for a reader instrument.

## F1b · The Ehrenberg-Bass evidence base — also weaker than its reputation

**What E-B actually established, peer-reviewed:** buyers of competing brands look alike **on demographics** in mature CPG — [Hammond, Ehrenberg & Goodhardt 1996](https://doi.org/10.1108/03090569610153291) (**observational panel**, 20+ grocery categories: profiles "differ little" on socio-demographics). Double jeopardy is directionally robust across categories ([Ehrenberg, Goodhardt & Barwise 1990](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224299005400307)) but modest in size (penetration differences dwarf loyalty differences) with acknowledged exceptions (niche and change-of-pace brands) — and **the entire corpus is observational CPG panel data; there is no field experiment anywhere in it**.

**What E-B claims but has not established:**
- The famous attitudes result — Kennedy & Ehrenberg's "There Is No Brand Segmentation" (~2–3pp brand-profile deviations across 43 industries × 100–200 attitudinal variables) — sits in a **practitioner magazine and a conference proceeding neither of two research passes could obtain**. The single most-cited number in the E-B segmentation case traces to an unobtainable grey-literature document.
- **E-B has never tested personality.** No study in the corpus tests whether users of competing brands differ on a validated personality inventory (the two look-alike papers are about *brand* personality — traits ascribed to brands, not buyers). Its one intervention on psychological targeting was the data-free Matz rebuttal letter, which required a [published correction](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1814739115) to disclose funding from Facebook and its competitors.
- The published critiques land on the exact crux: [Allenby et al. 2002](https://doi.org/10.1023/A:1020226922683) (13-author position paper) — E-B tested context-free trait descriptors, and "no differences in who buys" ≠ "no differences in what message works"; [Yang, Allenby & Fennell 2002](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.21.1.14.159) (n = 842) — swap demographics for *motivating conditions* and brand preference **is** heterogeneous; [Sheth & Koschmann 2019](https://doi.org/10.1108/ejm-07-2018-0489) (n ≈ 17,700 households) — 85–97% quarter-over-quarter brand persistence in cola, on E-B's home turf.

**The best-identified targeting evidence belongs to neither camp:** [Neumann, Tucker, Subramanyam & Marshall 2023](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11129-023-09268-7) (**FIELD**, five studies, 102.6M impressions, open access): **third-party psychographic/probabilistic targeting segments performed *worse than random*** (9.4–11.0% accuracy vs a 16.0% random baseline) because the underlying data is simply wrong (~17% job-title accuracy) — while **first-party contextual data outperformed at a 9.35× benefit-cost ratio**. For a product that collects its own onboarding data, this is the finding that actually bears on the decision: PLY's data-quality position is the good cell of that table; its *sample-size* position is the problem (F8).

**So for PLY:** neither grand narrative survives its audit intact. E-B's "segmentation is illusory" is an extrapolation from demographics; Matz's "targeting is big" is a contested result whose largest study found nothing on clicks. The memo's resolution stands on method, not camp membership: at PLY's N, main effects are measurable and interactions are not, and that asymmetry — not either ideology — is what allocates the budget to craft.

## F1c · The creative-quality vendor numbers are also weak — do not switch citations, switch method

An audit of the creative-primacy vendors (**VENDOR** throughout):
- **Nielsen Catalina "creative = 47–49% of sales lift"** traces to a single pie chart in a 2017 ARF conference deck; the entire public method disclosure is one sentence ("we then modeled the proportional contribution of the five keys"). It is a residual attribution, not a variance decomposition, not causal, not peer-reviewed — US CPG only, ~450–500 campaigns, short-term lift only. Its own primary source contradicts the headline for TV: **media 50%, creative 37%**; the 47% average is carried by digital.
- **Paul Dyson's "creative quality ×12 multiplier"** is a *different mathematical object* — a best-to-worst ROI range ratio where within-brand ROI variation is *assumed* to be creative; it and the NCS figure get cited as corroborating each other but are not commensurable. The most useful verified line in Dyson's own top-10 list: **targeting is the smallest lever, at ×1.1**.
- **Binet & Field**: the dependent variable behind the famous charts is a 3-point self-graded scale on paid, self-submitted IPA award entries. The flagship "creatively awarded work is 11–12× more efficient" was **7× on the full sample, rested on 29 awarded campaigns**, and per the IPA's own 2019 *Crisis in Creative Effectiveness* has since fallen to **parity** with non-awarded work.
- **System1**: the famous r = 0.83 (ESOV × Star Rating → share growth) is **n = 12 brands, one UK cereal category, in-sample, frozen since 2018, published in an investor deck** (95% CI ≈ [0.49, 0.95]); the "0.5pp → 3pp share per 10pp ESOV" is a bucketed average whose top bucket is 4% of ads. **Nelson-Field's** platform correlation is r = .97 on **five data points**. No out-of-sample validation exists anywhere in either body of work; two widely-cited documents ("The Crown Jewels", "Viewability, Attention and Sales") **do not appear to exist**. [System1 methodology](https://system1group.com/methodology) · [ThinkTV deck](https://thinktv.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/10/Jon-Evans-Emotion.pdf).
- **Why no credible split exists:** [Lewis & Rao 2015, QJE](https://gwern.net/doc/economics/advertising/2015-lewis.pdf) (**FIELD**, 25 experiments) — the median CI on a single campaign's ROI is **>100 percentage points wide**; [Shapiro, Hitsch & Tuchman 2021, Econometrica](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w27684/w27684.pdf) (288 brands) — two-thirds of TV ad elasticities indistinguishable from zero. If one campaign's effect can't be pinned within a factor of 2–3, a five-way percentage split reported to single-digit precision is decorative.
- **What survives the audit:** [Lodish et al. 1995, JMR](https://doi.org/10.1177/002224379503200201) (**FIELD**, 389 split-cable RCTs, the one experimental anchor — abstract-verified, full text paywalled): increasing media weight alone "does not increase sales in general," while copy/strategy changes work conditionally — copy variation genuinely beats incremental exposure. And Dyson's own list puts audience-targeting refinement at **×1.1, the smallest lever he measures** ([Thinkbox 2023, primary](https://assets.ctfassets.net/ptzdhtf6t0jg/2MUC3PzEg3yJPAvXidtXbt/75c0a9831f6d082877c7ee975f6bc591/The_Round_Up_2023.pdf)). The directional claim "copy > weight > targeting refinement" survives; every specific "creative is X%" number does not.

**So for PLY:** resolve the controversy by *method*, not by citation count. The peer-reviewed field evidence says targeting effects are small and fragile; the vendor evidence for creative primacy is directionally plausible but statistically thin. What survives both audits: creative/copy main effects are **measurable with far less data than interactions** (a main effect needs ~1/4 the sample of a same-sized interaction, and interactions run ~half the size of main effects — Gelman's 16×, decomposed in F8). Craft wins on *detectability and actionability at our N*, which is the decision that matters, independent of whose grand narrative is right.

## F2 · Peter Fader, answered directly

**Fader's heterogeneity is real, large, and continuous — and it is entirely about *propensity*, never about creative response. Invoking him for content-targeting is a category error; invoking him for a continuous fire-propensity model is exactly on-target.** Confidence: high (his full CV — ~70 refereed articles — was read; the boundary is a verified clean negative).

- The BTYD tradition ([Fader, Hardie & Lee 2005](https://www.brucehardie.com/papers/bgnbd_2004-04-20.pdf); [Fader & Hardie 2009 tutorial](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2012/04/Fader_hardie_jim_09.pdf)) models individuals as simple stochastic processes with **continuous mixing distributions** (gamma, beta) over latent rates. His signature result: aggregate patterns that *look like* distinct types or changing behavior are generated by **a smooth continuous distribution plus selection** ("sorting effect in a heterogeneous population where individual customers exhibit no retention-rate dynamics").
- [Fader & Hardie 2010, "Perils of Ignoring Heterogeneity"](http://www.brucehardie.com/papers/022/fader_hardie_mksc_10.pdf): ignoring continuous heterogeneity biases valuations 25–50%. Heterogeneity matters enormously — *as propensity*.
- [Fader, Hardie, McCarthy & Vaidyanathan 2019](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/1027/1/Hardie_Exploring_2018-10-16.pdf): two different continuous mixing distributions fit identically, and **more flexible ones did worse out-of-sample**. The *shape* of heterogeneity is weakly identified even with good data — you can estimate *that* readers differ and *how much*, not *what kind*.
- The exhaustive negative: **nothing in his corpus on copy, framing, tone, or trait×message interactions.** His only creative-adjacent paper ([Schwartz, Bradlow & Fader 2017](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2016.1023), display-ad bandits, **FIELD**) models heterogeneity across *placements*, not viewer types.
- His persona critique is real but lives in interviews, not papers ([Knowledge@Wharton](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/peter-fader-on-customer-centricity-and-why-it-matters/)): segment on **future value**, not demographics or stereotypes. No primary-source "segments are a management convenience" quote exists — that framing is our extrapolation, labeled as such.

**So for PLY:** the Fader-shaped move at N=50 is a **beta-binomial over reader fire-propensity** — two parameters, estimable now, with per-reader shrinkage doing exactly the right thing — plus the discipline to expect apparent "reader types" to be sorting artifacts. This is also precisely the pickiness/scale-use covariate the calibration step already planned. Continuous propensity vector: yes, and cheap. Named archetypes: his work is the argument *against* them.

## F3 · Archetype systems with measured conversion: there are none

**Charles's question answered directly: no marketing archetype/typology system has a published randomized test showing that matching creative to type raises conversion.** Confidence: high.

| System | Best available number | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| VALS | adj. R² = .060–.085 predicting one behavior ([Novak, de Leeuw & MacEvoy 1992](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/7gg3c40h), n = 2,591 — authored by the vendor's own scientist, who calls it an "upper bound") | Descriptive segmentation; zero conversion experiments in ~40 years |
| PRIZM / Mosaic | OR 1.59–2.30 extreme-group on *health outcomes* ([Bright et al. 2021](https://academic.oup.com/jpubhealth/article/43/4/797/5891385), observational, n ≈ 69k) | Real but modest descriptive signal; **zero matched-creative lift tests** |
| Jungian brand archetypes (Mark & Pearson) | "97% more MVA growth" — **n = 50 brands, author-coded, retrospective, in a book, never peer-reviewed or replicated**; peer-reviewed work finds strong brands use *multiple* archetypes ([Cortez et al. 2022](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0007681322000222)) | A writer's voice tool, not a measured reader classifier |
| System1 | r = 0.83 at n = 12 brands, in-sample, vendor deck (F1c) | **Contains no person-typology at all** — it measures viewer emotion about the *ad* and audits *ad* features |

The constructs that *do* carry measured matching effects are not archetypes: health-message tailoring (**META**, [Noar, Benac & Harris 2007](http://pham315.pbworks.com/f/Noar+et+al+2007.pdf), 57 studies, N = 58,454): tailored vs comparison message **r = .058, d = 0.12, OR = 1.21** — the honest expected value of message-tailoring after 30 years. Regulatory fit ([Motyka et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2013.11.004), **META**, mostly LAB): direction confirmed, weaker on behavior than evaluation — and **its abstract contains no overall effect size; the "d = 0.30" circulating for it could not be traced to any readable source**. No field experiments of regulatory fit in real advertising exist. A 2025 preregistered replication effort (Ścigała et al., N = 3,150) found no support for the fit interaction it tested.

**So for PLY:** the prior session's instinct to reject borrowed typologies was correct — but the same verdict applies to any *invented* one, including the eight-asset taxonomy (see Verdicts). If a taxonomy of readers with measured conversion existed, this sweep would have found it. It found d = 0.12.

## F4 · Large-N headline experiments: small effects, choice-set-relative — and three of our tone rules are empirically confirmed

Confidence: high on the findings, medium on transfer (see the transfer caveat).

The [Upworthy Research Archive](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-021-00934-7) (**FIELD**, 32,487 real A/B tests, 150,817 headlines, 538M assignments; note: 22% of tests had a randomization bug and are excluded from confirmatory work per the [2024 correction](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-024-03600-w)):

- [Robertson et al. 2023, *Nature Human Behaviour*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01538-4) (preregistered, confirmatory sample, 5.7M clicks): each negative word **+2.3% relative CTR — on a 1.39% base, that is +0.03 percentage points**. Positive words −1.0%. **Length was the largest coefficient in the model** (longer won). Upworthy's own style decayed hard within 27 months (platform-age β = −0.309).
- **Negativity does not transfer.** The same lab's follow-up across **398 news sites, >150,000 A/B tests** ([Banerjee & Urminsky](https://akshinabanerjee.com/s/Rethinking-the-Negativity-Bias-Final-Mansucript.pdf), **FIELD + 5 preregistered lab studies**, N = 8,369): negativity **null on average**, wildly heterogeneous across platforms (~90% of the heterogeneity unexplained), and moderated by reader goal — **negative/engineered language backfires for credibility-seeking readers, mediated by inferences of manipulative intent**. A person deciding whether to meet a stranger is a maximally credibility-seeking reader. *This is the study that says PLY's tone rules are the right copy policy for this reader, not just a taste preference.*
- [Banerjee & Urminsky's 36-construct decomposition](https://home.uchicago.edu/ourminsky/Banerjee_Urminsky_Headlines.pdf) (draft; published in *Marketing Science* 2025 — construct directions verified to draft only, flagged): of 21 constructs with clear prior-literature predictions, the direction matched for **7 and ran opposite for 14** — copywriting folklore is a coin flip. Bonferroni-surviving positives: **story about one specific individual (+6.4%/SD, the largest effect in the paper — literally PLY's format)**, concreteness, forward-reference (a concrete unresolved detail — *not* a question), numbers, emotional intensity, "disfluency" (longer/harder text). Bonferroni-surviving *negatives*: **addressing the reader** ("writer to reader"), **information-seeking/question framing**, **positive-emotion piling**, present tense.
- Concreteness is non-monotonic and choice-set-relative: [Aubin Le Quéré & Matias 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-81575-9) (**META of field experiments**, 8,977 tests, image held constant): the *sign* of the concreteness effect **flips depending on the concreteness of competing headlines in the same test**. CTR is maximized at middling concreteness.
- Base rates for humility: across the general A/B literature, only **20–26% of experiments produce a significant effect** ([Berman et al.](https://ron-berman.com/papers/fdr.pdf), 2,766 Optimizely experiments); Bing effect sizes are power-law fat-tailed with the **top 2% of ideas producing 74.8% of all gains** ([Azevedo et al. 2020, JPE](https://eduardomazevedo.github.io/papers/azevedo-et-al-ab.pdf)); "only one third of ideas tested at Microsoft improved the metrics they were designed to improve" ([Kohavi et al. 2013](https://exp-platform.com/Documents/2013%20controlledExperimentsAtScale.pdf)).

**The transfer caveat, which is itself a finding:** every Upworthy effect is estimated *within a test, against competing arms*, on a ~1.4% base. PLY's reader sees **one card and no rival**. At least one major variable's sign is set by the competitive context (concreteness above). **No published study bridges the single-offer vs multi-option estimand** — this is a genuine gap, and the reason Phase R's own randomization is worth more than any imported coefficient.

**So for PLY:** the tone rules survive contact with the largest click corpora that exist — three of them (no reader address, no rhetorical questions, no sentiment piling) are independently confirmed as CTR-*negative* devices, and the format itself (one named individual, concrete, story-shaped) sits on the largest measured positive. The craft variables worth testing are in the ranked list below. Nothing in these corpora supports reader-typed content aiming.

## F5 · Dating-specific text effects

Confidence: medium-high (the format itself is unstudied; adjacent evidence is strong).

- **The famous "photos dominate" citation is a misattribution.** [Hitsch, Hortaçsu & Ariely 2010](https://www.tau.ac.il/~weiss/fam_econ/Matching%20and%20Sorting%20in%20Online%20Dating%20-%20Hitsch.pdf) (**PLATFORM**, N ≈ 5,800 users, 439k first-contact decisions) *excluded profile text entirely* ("too unstructured"). What it does show: photo-decile effects on first contact of roughly **3–5×** bottom-to-top, and men and women weighting looks **almost identically** (0.5604 vs 0.5848) — the real gender gap was income (~3×). In photo-present apps, photos dominate; PLY's pre-photo fire removes exactly that dominant term.
- The study that actually decomposed it ([Fiore et al. 2008, CHI](https://doi.org/10.1145/1357054.1357181), **LAB**): photo > free text >> **fixed-choice fields (null)**. Structured attributes contributed nothing; prose did. Eye-tracking ([van der Zanden et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650221995316), **LAB**): text receives attention *regardless* of picture attractiveness; pictures and text are processed independently.
- What makes profile TEXT work ([van der Zanden et al. 2022, PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0274860), **observational rating study**, 1,234 raters × 308 real texts): originality → perceived intelligence (b = .56) and humor (b = .45) → attraction; features that read as original: **word count (β = .41), self-disclosing statements (β = .37), concrete self-disclosure (β = .24), novel metaphors (β = .15)**. And the biggest single coefficient is negative: **oddness → −0.52** — the downside of overshooting novelty exceeds the upside of landing it; a follow-up experiment (N = 295) concludes **appropriateness beats novelty**.
- Demonstrated humor moves choices ([Bressler & Balshine 2006](https://abel.mcmaster.ca/publications/pdfs/Bressler%20-%20Balshine.pdf), **LAB**, N = 210, written statements): women chose humor-producing men ~30% above chance — while rating them *less* intelligent and trustworthy. Production, not claims; and the trait side-effects argue for PLY's route (wit via specifics and originality, not joke-telling).
- **Warranting theory is the format's best theoretical support:** other-generated claims beat self-claims ([Walther et al. 2009](https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650208330251), **LAB**, Exp 2: friends' comments overrode self-claims on attractiveness; [Utz 2010](https://academic.oup.com/jcmc/article-pdf/15/2/314/19441731/jjcmcom0314.pdf): only other-generated information moved social attraction). A narrator describing a stranger is structurally other-generated. **But the matchmaker-card format itself has never been tested** — no published outcome data exists for Hinge friend-endorsements, Ship, Tawkify, or any third-person intro product. PLY will generate the first real dataset on its own format.
- First messages ([OkCupid, >500k contacts](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/okcupid/exactlywhattosayinafirstmessage.html), **PLATFORM**, uncontrolled): specific niche interests help ("every 'niche' word we have significant data on has a positive effect"), physical compliments hurt, netspeak worst. [Bruch & Newman 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6082652/) (**PLATFORM**): longer messages are *not* generally rewarded; "variation in payoff for different strategies is fairly small."
- **The pre-meeting stage is where text-based similarity cues peak** — [Montoya et al. 2008](https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407508096700) (**META**, 313 studies): actual similarity r = **.59 in no-interaction settings** → .21 short interaction → **.08 n.s.** in relationships (breakdown verified secondary via Tidwell et al.'s citation; SAGE blocked the primary). And ideals-matched written profiles produced large pre-meeting effects that **vanished after four minutes of live contact** ([Eastwick, Finkel & Eagly 2011](https://elifinkel.squarespace.com/s/67_EastwickFinkelEagly2011_JPSP.pdf), **LAB**, two experiments, p = .001 both). This looks like targeting evidence — until [Sparks et al. 2020](https://elifinkel.com/s/InPress_SparksDalyWilkeyMoldenFinkelEastwick_JESP.pdf) (**LAB/field**, N = 128 blind dates + 598): possessing *your own* stated ideals (β = .46) predicted interest no better than possessing *someone else's* (β = .36); **unique-fit test null (p = .690)**. Good qualities are good for everyone. The "matching" effect is mostly a craft main effect wearing a targeting costume.
- **Amendment (2026-08-24): the Sparks deflation applies to stated-ideal matching, not to pair actual similarity — the first draft blurred the two.** Sparks tested possession of *ideal traits* (things people list as wanted in a partner), which are mostly universally valued — that is why anyone's list works. Actual similarity ("we share attribute X") is **irreducibly pair-relative**: in the bogus-stranger paradigm behind Montoya's no-interaction cell, the *same* stimulus profile attracts a similar reader and repels a dissimilar one — a genuine reader × content interaction, and the only matching axis in this sweep with a large measured effect in exactly PLY's setting. Three honesty caveats before it carries weight: (a) the r ≈ .47–.59 is **LAB-attitudinal** (attitude checklists as the whole stimulus, attraction scales as the DV) — the nearest *behavioral* tests of actual similarity are null ([Tidwell et al. 2013](https://elifinkel.squarespace.com/s/2013_TidwellEastwickFinkel_PersonalRelationships.pdf): β = .005 across 26 speed-dating analyses; post-meeting, so consistent with the decay curve, but it means no behavioral demonstration exists at any stage — expect the fire-rate version to be far smaller than the lab number; (b) the causal route is **perceived** similarity (r = .39, the one similarity effect stable across settings; Tidwell's defensible causal estimate β = .069) — and since Rule 9 bars reader reference, the card can never *say* "like you"; the reader must recognize the overlap unaided, so the effect is bounded by recognition, favoring identity-salient overlap dimensions (tribe, banter register) over demographic ones (education); (c) a similarity-*lead* factor manipulates **emphasis, not similarity level** — no published study tests whether surfacing a true overlap first beats leading with the best material; that specific contrast is mechanism-consistent but undemonstrated. Net: pair-overlap aiming is a hypothesis *with* a mechanism — categorically better-founded than trait×style targeting, and the honest reading of Anne/Jessica: the salvageable version of that intuition is a fact about **pairs** (what each shares with Dane), not about reader types.

**So for PLY:** text can carry the decision — the null results people cite (algorithms can't predict compatibility; no gender differences post-meeting) are all measured *one stage after* the moment PLY optimizes. The levers are craft levers: concrete self-disclosure, originality capped by appropriateness, demonstrated (never claimed) wit, verbatim quotes as warranting. Reader-typed content aiming appears nowhere in this literature's measured effects.

## F6 · Framing fit: not a better-evidenced targeting axis

Confidence: high. Regulatory-focus fit: **META** exists but publishes no overall effect size ([Motyka et al. 2014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcps.2013.11.004)); nearly all LAB; the only field-adjacent results sit at p = .05–.055 with N ≈ 130–206; a preregistered 2025 replication (N = 3,150) found nothing; **zero field experiments in real advertising**. Construal-level fit: no meta-analysis exists; the underlying distance→abstraction effect is under active overestimation critique (Maier et al. 2022). Emotional-vs-rational: main-effect SMD ≈ 0.105, sign *flips* across cultures. Personalization cues: the one large clean field result is **name insertion in email subject lines** ([Sahni, Wheeler & Chintagunta 2018](https://doi.org/10.1287/mksc.2017.1066), **FIELD**: open rate 9.05%→10.80%, leads +31%) — which is identity-recognition, not psychological matching, and is unavailable to PLY (the card never addresses the reader — a rule F4 now supports empirically). Personalization backfires under privacy salience (creepiness/reactance, **LAB**).

**So for PLY:** no framing-fit axis has better field evidence than the Big Five axis Matz used — and that one is contested and small. The framing literature's transferable content is its *backfire conditions*: fit perceived as engineered triggers correction (Cesario et al. 2004; Kim et al. 2019), converging with the manipulative-intent mechanism in F4. Aiming that is visible is aiming that costs.

## F7 · The Goodhart problem: the guardrail is right, and the risk is structural

Confidence: high on direction. Measured anchors:
- [Norton, Frost & Ariely 2007](https://people.duke.edu/~dandan/webfiles/PapersUpside/Less%20is%20More.pdf) (**FIELD survey**, N = 118 real online daters): liking **7.08 → 5.13** (10-pt scale) from pre-date to post-date; 81–88% of people wrongly believe more information increases liking; more traits known → less liking (mediated by perceived dissimilarity). Their caveat cuts both ways: high expectations cost disappointment but "the lone payoff may be worth the many disappointments." [Reis et al. 2011](https://doi.org/10.1037/a0022885) (**LAB**) contest the mechanism for *live interaction* (more interaction → more liking via responsiveness) — the reconciliation: **static attribute lists about an absent person degrade with volume; responsive interaction builds.** A pitch card is the former. Brevity is protective; the 3–4 sentence cap is evidence-aligned.
- [Toma & Hancock 2008](https://www.cl.cam.ac.uk/events/shb/2009/hancock2.pdf) (**FIELD**, N = 80, claims vs measured reality): 81% of daters deviate but magnitudes are small (height 2.09%, weight 5.5%) — because **anticipated face-to-face contact caps self-exaggeration. A system-written pitch has no such governor** — the writer never sits across the table. PLY must supply the governor institutionally: the exact-verbatim quote rule and the accuracy flag are that governor.
- [Milli et al. 2025, PNAS Nexus](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16941) (**FIELD**, preregistered, N = 806): engagement-ranked content rated *slightly higher* overall but significantly *lower* for the contested category — engagement–satisfaction divergence is real, category-specific, not a blanket law. [Kleinberg, Mullainathan & Raghavan](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11776) prove divergence *can* be total; they do not show it *is*. Clickbait's downstream costs are weaker than folklore: [Munger et al. 2020](https://academic.oup.com/poq/article/84/1/49/5786916) found **null** effects on trust and learning. No study measures click-optimized dating algorithms degrading relationship outcomes — that claim is punditry.
- [Ramirez & Wang 2008](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1460-2466.2007.00372.x) (**LAB**): the face-to-face transition is judged *more negatively* the longer the text-only phase runs — supports fast photo reveal and fast dates after a fire, not a long courtship of cards.

**So for PLY:** photo-interest-given-fire per style (G2) is the correct guardrail and the literature adds its calibration: the failure mode is not "clickbait erodes trust in the app" (weakly evidenced) but **expectation inflation burning pairs under Rule 1 permanence** (directly evidenced, 2-point drop on a 10-point scale). Add the date-report guardrail when volume allows: fire→date conversion per style, not just fire→photo. And keep cards short — volume of claims is itself a Goodhart risk.

## F8 · Heterogeneity, decomposed — the three things the prior session ran together

Confidence: high (this is mostly arithmetic plus the strongest field data in the sweep).

**(a) Propensity heterogeneity — real, large, and dominant.** Fader's whole corpus (F2); activity-bias literature; and the strongest single dataset: [Gordon et al. 2019, *Marketing Science*](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/gordon_b/files/kellogg_fb_whitepaper.pdf) (**FIELD**, 12 Facebook lift studies, 435M observations): observational exposed-vs-unexposed comparisons overstated a true 77% lift as 416% — **person-level selection swamps content effects even after conditioning on thousands of behavioral variables.** An illustrative variance decomposition calibrated to the literature (assumption-labeled inputs, scripts in repo notes): reader propensity ≈ 76% of systematic variance, card craft ≈ 19%, framing main effect ≈ 3%, **reader × content interaction ≈ 1%** — and the interaction share stays ≈ 0.2–1.2% across the plausible range.

**(b) Response-to-creative heterogeneity — the contested one, and the small one.** Everything above: realistic r ≈ .14–.17, behavioral-interaction evidence resting on 5–15 studies, best head-to-head split 1–1 with "best single message," unique-fit null in the dating-adjacent lab.

**(c) Subject main effect — large.** Photo-decile first-contact effects of 3–5× (Hitsch); in speed-dating SRM, partner variance (22–26%) roughly doubles actor variance (12–14%) ([Joel, Eastwick & Finkel 2017](https://elifinkel.com/s/132_JoelEastwickFinkel2017_PSci.pdf)). **T-SIM's assumption that candidate variance dominates reader-style variance is supported** — with the caveat that pre-meeting *text-only* variance decomposition has never been measured; PLY's `candidate (random)` term will produce the first real estimate.

**Detectability, computed for PLY's actual design** (50 readers × ~24 pitches, fire base ~20–25%, within-reader randomization — the favorable case):

| Target | Minimum detectable at 80% power | Literature-calibrated effect | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Style/lead **main effect** | OR ≈ 1.47 (~10pp) | craft variables live here | **detectable** |
| Reader × content **interaction** | **OR ≈ 2.24 (~13pp DiD)** | OR ≈ 1.67 (from r = .14–.17) | **below the floor** |
| `content_targeting` aimed-vs-random arm | needs ~3,400+ events (the random arm matches by chance half the time, halving the observable gap) | ~3–4pp | **far below the floor** |

Median power to detect typical interactions in *published psychology* is 0.18 ([Sommet et al. 2023](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/25152459231178728), 159-study metastudy); [Gelman's 16×](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2018/03/15/need16/) decomposes as 4× pure statistics (interaction SE is 2× the main-effect SE) times 4× typical-effect-ratio; and his Type-M result: significant interaction estimates at 10% power average **3× the true value**. At ~100 readers in the within-reader design, a moderate interaction (OR 2.0) reaches ~80% power — that is the honest threshold at which targeting becomes a testable hypothesis rather than a faith commitment.

## Verdict on the eight-asset taxonomy

**Unsupported as a reader-classification instrument. Salvageable as a writer's checklist.** The taxonomy commits the exact sin the archetypes brief convicted Jungian archetypes of: it is a *writer's* instrument (a useful inventory of what raw material a pitch can be built from — the line, the image, the long thing, the tell, the wrong turn, the witness, the noticing, the care) being asked to do a *reader-classification* job no measured framework has ever done (F3). There is no evidence that stable per-reader asset-class preferences exist, no instrument validated to measure them, and no power to detect them at launch scale even if they exist (F8). The `asset_priors` 8-vector from a redesigned taste step would be eight noisy numbers per reader that nothing at launch can validly consume.

**What replaces it:** (1) keep the asset vocabulary as *generation-side* material tags — log `lead_asset` and `assets_available[]` on every pitch as free retrospective covariates, which costs nothing and banks the data honestly; (2) the reader side carries a **continuous propensity model** (beta-binomial pickiness + scale-use, already in the calibration spec) instead of a preference typology; (3) the taste step stays a *style/quality* calibration and pickiness instrument, not an asset-preference instrument — do not redesign it around the taxonomy.

## Verdict on the Anne/Jessica claim

**Unsupported — an intuition with the evidence pointing the other way.** The claim was that Anne (fashion, urbane) and Jessica (gourmand) differ enough in *what content converts them* that the same Dane needs differently-aimed cards. Everything measured says: (a) both will respond primarily through their own fire-propensity and Dane's overall appeal, which jointly dwarf any content-matching term (F8a/c); (b) the qualities that move readers are substantially universal — someone else's ideals predict interest as well as your own (Sparks, unique-fit null, F5); (c) if a residual Anne-vs-Jessica content interaction exists, its literature-calibrated size (r ≈ .14 ceiling) is undetectable below ~100 readers, and any launch-scale "detection" of it would be ~3× inflated (F8); and (d) aiming a mediocre card at Anne is predicted to do *worse* than showing her the best card (Teeny, F1/F6). Charles's skeptical question — "do Jessica and Anne differ really that much?" — is the position the evidence supports. Write the carburetor into the card when the carburetor makes the best card, for everyone.

## Ranked testable copy variables for the pitch generator

Variables with measured effect elsewhere that survive the nine tone rules and could be randomized or logged in Phase R. Ranked by (evidence strength × transfer plausibility × detectability as a main effect). The first three are already tone rules — the news is they're now *empirically backed*, which upgrades them from taste to policy.

1. **Concrete specificity (calibrated, not maximal)** — field-backed twice over ([Packard & Berger 2021](https://academic.oup.com/jcr/article/47/5/787/5873524): +8.9% satisfaction, +13–30% spend; mechanism = *signals attention to this specific person*, which is the pitch's whole job) and Bonferroni-surviving in Upworthy. Caveat: inverted-U — middling beats maximal (F4). Already Rule 8; make the critic score it. **Log-only at launch** (concreteness score per card), randomize a high-vs-mid concreteness contrast later.
2. **Verbatim quote, visibly marked** — warranting theory (other-generated > self-claimed) + humor-production (Bressler & Balshine) + the "line" asset surfaced as demonstration rather than claim. This is the strongest *new* craft mechanic the literature supports. Randomizable as a clean binary: **quote-marked vs quote-woven**, same quote, same card otherwise. Needs the exact-string critic check and the §8 privacy ruling first.
3. **Forward-reference close** — a concrete unresolved detail as the last line ("ask him which of the three is hardest; the answer changes monthly") — Bonferroni-positive in Upworthy, +16pp in Kuiken, and it is the tone-rule-legal version of curiosity (the question-framed version is empirically *negative*). Randomizable: forward-reference close vs image close.
4. **Length within the cap** — longer beat shorter in both Upworthy analyses (largest coefficient in Robertson) *but* volume of claims degrades liking in the dating context (Norton). A clean 3-sentence vs 4-sentence randomization resolves which regime a pitch card lives in. Cheap, well-powered, genuinely uncertain — a good early experiment.
5. **Originality with an appropriateness governor** — novel metaphor and unexpected phrasing raise perceived intelligence and humor (β = .15–.41 paths), but oddness carries −0.52; appropriateness dominates novelty. Operationalize as a critic check (flag cards a median reader would call odd), not a randomized axis.
6. **The four angles and two leads — keep, as craft variation, not as targeting.** Nothing here invalidates randomizing angle/lead to find *global* winners (main effects are detectable; a best-single-message strategy needs exactly this data — Tappin). What changes: drop the expectation that trait×angle interactions will be readable at launch (E-STYLE's H1/H2 should be re-labeled Phase-T hypotheses), and treat sequencing's per-reader learning as a long-horizon benefit, not a launch deliverable.
7. **Breach/tension present vs absent** — tellability literature and Rule 3 agree; log the Labov `breach` boolean per card (already specced in story-elicitation) and analyze retrospectively before spending randomization budget on it.
8. **Similarity lead (`similarity_lead`, per `specs/matching-v2-content-targeting.md`)** — lead with a dimension where the pair actually overlaps, vs best-material lead. The one targeting-flavored variable that survives this memo's audit, because it is pair-level actual similarity (F5 amendment), not a reader typology or stated-ideal matching. Randomized from day one under the backfill rule; readout event-gated. Two design requirements from the evidence: **craft parity between arms** (the aimed cell constrains the lead to overlap material, which may be weaker than the subject's best material — per Teeny's amplification result, an aimed lead on weak material underperforms for craft reasons and would masquerade as a similarity null; the critic threshold must be identical in both arms, and critic scores logged per arm so a quality gap is visible in the readout), and **log realized overlap in the random arm** (`lead_dimension ∈ overlap_dimensions` — the random arm will match by chance, diluting the assigned contrast; analyzing by realized overlap-of-lead with assignment as the instrument recovers the lost power).

Anti-variables, empirically confirmed (enforce, don't test): reader address, question framing, sentiment piling/positivity, physical-appearance compliments, netspeak/errors (floor condition), claim-stacking beyond ~4 sentences.

## What the literature cannot tell us — PLY's own measurement agenda

1. **The single-offer estimand.** Every large click corpus measures choice *among competing headlines*; at least one major variable's sign flips with the choice set. Nobody has measured copy effects on a one-card-no-rival decision. Phase R's randomization is the first dataset on this — which is why imported coefficients are priors, never substitutes.
2. **The matchmaker format.** Third-person narrative introduction of a real stranger, pre-photo, has zero published outcome data anywhere. Warranting theory predicts it works; nobody has tested it. PLY is the experiment.
3. **Pre-meeting text-only variance decomposition** — how fire variance actually splits across reader propensity / candidate appeal / card craft / interaction. The `reader (random) + candidate (random)` terms in the pre-registered model produce the first real numbers; they are more valuable than any style contrast and should be reported in every readout.
4. **Whether reader×content interactions exist at all in this format** — answerable honestly at ~100+ readers (within-reader design, moderate-interaction power ≈ 80%), not at 50. Bank `reader_traits`, `asset` tags, and calibration votes now; test then. Detection floor and Type-M inflation go in the pre-registration so the future readout can't oversell itself.
5. **Fire → photo → date attrition by style** — the Goodhart chain (F7). The literature gives direction (expectation inflation is the real risk) but no rates for this format; G2 plus a fire→date guardrail measure it.
